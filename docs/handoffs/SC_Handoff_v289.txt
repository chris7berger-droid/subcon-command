SESSION SUMMARY
Office Time Clock can review recorded punches, open an employee’s day, and
export the same review as CSV. Calculated work hours and a separate drive
duration are on the screen. Regular, OT, and double time stay blank.
Add, edit, and void are visible and unavailable. No merge, no production

IMPLEMENTED
The existing range reader is unchanged in behavior: inclusive stored
punch_date, both dates required, Pacific today as the default, id filters,
missing joins kept, pages ordered through punch id, and a newer range ignoring
an older response. The query also reads two days on either side so an
overnight clock-out can close a shift. Displayed punches stay inside the
selected punch_date range.

The table is employee-first. Job number and job name are separate columns.
The large punch-count card is a compact count. Opening a day shows that
shift’s punches in time order, the stored punch_date, and the full Pacific
timestamp. Stored regular, OT, and drive stay as stored: zero stays visible,
null stays blank. Calculated hours are not written back.

One calculator feeds the screen and the CSV. A shift belongs to the Pacific
date of its clock-in. A closed shift with a lunch start deducts exactly 30
unpaid minutes. A late or missing lunch_end does not change that deduction
and does not invent a punch. A deduction that would go negative is flagged
and left blank. Overlaps, unfamiliar events inside a shift, and a missing
clock-out are flagged and left blank. Drive duration is separate. Its pay
and overtime treatment are not confirmed. Status for a calculated shift is
“Classification pending”. The CSV leaves regular, OT, and double time blank.
Text cells are quoted, and a leading = + - or @ is prefixed so a spreadsheet
does not execute it.

UNAVAILABLE
Add, edit, and void do not save. The preview reads production data. The
correction migration is not applied, and the screen does not call it.

The migration can, in Postgres, keep an office edit from being overwritten,
skip a voided id, let a different new punch insert, keep a mobile clock-in
moving Scheduled or Parked to In Progress, and keep an office clock-in from
doing that. A skipped void insert returns no row. The phone treats a failed
upload as a failed batch and retries it, which can block a new punch. A
successful no-op also does not update the copy stored on the phone. Those
two phone behaviors are the dependency. Writes stay off until both are
handled and Chris approves the migration.

Workweek start, premium rules, drive pay, provider templates, Buildertrend
import, pay rates, and work-type allocation are not in this build.
